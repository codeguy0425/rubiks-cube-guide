Did You Know?

Fascinating Cube Facts

The Rubik's Cube is full of surprises — here are some of the most mind-bending ones.

🧊

43 Quintillion Combinations

That's 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 — more than the number of stars in the known universe. Yet every scramble can be solved in 20 moves or fewer.

🕰️

Invented in 1974

Hungarian architect Ernő Rubik created the cube as a teaching tool for spatial geometry. He called it the "Magic Cube" and spent a month solving it for the first time.

🏆

World Record: 3.13s

Max Park holds the current world record for a single solve (3.13 seconds, June 2023). The record has dropped from 22.95s in 1982 to under 4s today.

📦

Best-Selling Toy Ever

Over 500 million cubes have been sold worldwide — making it the best-selling puzzle toy of all time. The original 1980s craze saw 200 million sold in 3 years.

🎯

God's Number: 20

Every possible Rubik's Cube scramble can be solved in 20 moves or fewer (half-turn metric). This was proven in 2010 by a team using Google's computers — 35 years after the cube's invention.

👁️

Blindfolded World Record

Tommy Cherry solved a cube blindfolded in 14.51 seconds (2024). Blindfolded solvers memorize the cube, put on a blindfold, then execute the solution from memory.

🤖

Robots Solve in Under 1s

The fastest robot can solve a cube in just 0.305 seconds. It uses cameras to detect the state and motors to execute the solution faster than the human eye can follow.

🌍

World Cube Association

The WCA governs official cubing competitions across 100+ countries with events for 17 different puzzle types — from the 2×2 to the infamous 7×7 cube.

The Language of Cubing

Cube Notation

Before you learn algorithms, you need to speak the language. Each letter represents a face turn.

U
Upper face clockwise
U'
Upper face counter-clockwise
D
Down face clockwise
D'
Down face counter-clockwise
R
Right face clockwise
R'
Right face counter-clockwise
L
Left face clockwise
L'
Left face counter-clockwise
F
Front face clockwise
F'
Front face counter-clockwise
B
Back face clockwise
B'
Back face counter-clockwise
A letter alone = clockwise. ' (prime) = counter-clockwise. 2 = double turn (e.g. R2).
Step by Step

Beginner: Layer by Layer

The most intuitive method for first-time solvers. We'll build the cube one layer at a time, from bottom to top.

1

The White Cross

Hold the cube with the white center on top. Find the four white edge pieces (pieces with white + one other color) and move them to the top layer, aligned with their matching center colors. Each edge should match both the white center and the side center.

  • Start by finding a white edge on the bottom layer and bringing it up
  • Align the side color of each white edge with the matching center
  • Make sure the white stickers face UP, forming a daisy pattern, then align and flip
2

White Corners

Hold the cube with white on top. Insert the four corner pieces to complete the first layer. Each corner goes in the front-top-right position. Move the piece you want to insert to the front-bottom-right corner, then apply the algorithm.

  • Find a white corner on the bottom layer
  • Position it at front-bottom-right — directly under where it needs to go (match the two side colors)
  • Apply: R' D' R D (repeat until solved)
  • If the white sticker is facing down, do the algorithm 4 times
  • If facing you, do it 2 times. If facing away, do it 3 times
  • If a white corner is already on top but in the wrong spot, pick any bottom corner and R' D' R D once to kick it down to the bottom layer, then re-insert it correctly
3

Middle Layer Edges

Flip the cube so the solved white layer is on the bottom. Insert the four middle layer edges. Find edges on the top layer that don't have yellow.

  • Match the front color of the edge to the corresponding center
  • If the edge needs to go to the right: U R U' R' U' F' U F
  • If the edge needs to go to the left: U' L' U L U F U' F'
  • If an edge is in the correct position but flipped, move it out using either algorithm, then re-insert it
4

The Yellow Cross

Hold the cube with yellow on top. Orient the top layer edges to form a yellow cross. Ignore the corners for now — only the four edge pieces matter.

  • Dot (no edges): F R U R' U' F' → will give you an "L" shape
  • L-shape (two adjacent edges): hold the L in the back-left corner, then F U R U' R' F'
  • Line (two opposite edges): hold the line horizontally, then F R U R' U' F'
5

Position Yellow Corners

Hold the cube with yellow on top. Now we position the four yellow corners into their correct spots (they might be rotated — that's fine). Look for two corners on the same side that match the side color.

  • If you have two matching corners on the same face, hold that face to the back and do: U R U' L' U R' U' L
  • If no corners match, do the algorithm from any angle once, then re-evaluate
  • After this step, all four corners should be in the correct position
6

Orient Yellow Corners

Hold the cube with yellow on top. Rotate the yellow corners so yellow faces up. Hold one unsolved corner in the front-right-top position.

  • Apply: R' D' R D (yes, the same algorithm from step 2)
  • Repeat until that corner's yellow sticker faces up (2 or 4 times)
  • Important: Don't re-grip the cube — just rotate the top layer (U turn) to bring the next unsolved corner into position
  • The bottom layers will get scrambled temporarily — keep going, they'll be restored!
7

Permute Yellow Edges

Hold the cube with yellow on top. Position the yellow edges to complete the cube. Look at the side colors of the top layer.

  • If one face is fully solved, hold it to the back and do: F2 U L R' F2 L' R U F2
  • If no face is fully solved, do the algorithm from any position, then re-evaluate
  • You may need to do this algorithm twice
  • Congratulations! You've solved your first Rubik's Cube! 🎉
Level Up

Intermediate: CFOP Method

Once you're comfortable with the beginner method, CFOP (pronounced "C-F-O-P" or "see-fop") is the next step to faster solving. It's the most popular speedcubing method used by world record holders.

Cross

Instead of building the white cross on top and flipping it down, build it directly on the bottom. This saves 2 moves and lets you see your first F2L pair during inspection.

Target: ≤ 8 moves

F2L — First Two Layers

Solve the white corner and middle layer edge simultaneously in one step. Instead of 3 separate steps in the beginner method, you insert corner-edge pairs together. 41 basic cases, but most are intuitive.

41 cases (mostly intuitive)

OLL — Orient Last Layer

Make the entire top face yellow in one algorithm. The beginner method uses 2 steps (cross + corners); full OLL does it in one. Start with "2-look OLL" (10 algorithms) before learning all 57.

57 algorithms

PLL — Permute Last Layer

Swap the top pieces into their correct positions to solve the cube. Like OLL, start with "2-look PLL" (6 algorithms) first. Full PLL gives you 21 algorithms for the fastest finishes.

21 algorithms
💡 Tip: Start with 2-look OLL (10 algs) + 2-look PLL (6 algs) = only 16 algorithms to reach sub-45 second solves.

Going Pro

Advanced Techniques

Once you're comfortable with CFOP, these techniques will push you past the plateau toward sub-15 and beyond.

⌛ Look Ahead

The most important skill for speed. During your current pair insertion, already be tracking where the next pair is — never pause between pairs. Practice slow-solving to develop this.

🖐️ Finger Tricks

Optimize how you push each face. Use index fingers for U and U' (push, not pull), wrist turns for F/F', and double-flick for U2. Efficient finger tricks save 0.3–0.5s per algorithm.

⚡ Advanced F2L

Learn to insert pairs from any angle, use empty slots, use sledgehammer (R' F R F'), and solve pairs when they're already connected but misoriented.

🧩 X-Cross

Build the cross plus one F2L pair during inspection. This skips a full insertion step and gives you a massive advantage. Start by planning the cross in inspection, then look for the easiest pair.

🎨 Full OLL / PLL

All 57 OLL and 21 PLL algorithms memorized. Learn them in small batches (5 per week) and drill each until it's muscle memory before adding more. Use Anki or similar for spaced repetition.

🌈 Color Neutrality

Don't always start with white — be comfortable building a cross on any color. This lets you choose the easiest scramble every time, saving 1–3 moves on cross. A long-term investment that pays off.
